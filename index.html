What is Probability?

Probability is a branch of mathematics that deals with chance and uncertainty. It helps us determine how likely an event is to happen. Every day, people make decisions based on probability, whether they realize it or not. For example, weather forecasters estimate the probability of rain, doctors assess the probability of recovery from a disease, insurance companies calculate the probability of accidents, and athletes analyze the probability of winning a game.

The value of probability ranges from 0 to 1 (or 0% to 100%), where:

  • 0 (or 0%) means an event is completely impossible.
  • 1 (or 100%) means an event is absolute certainty.
Probability Scale: 0 (Impossible) ───────── 0.5 (50% Equal Chance) ───────── 1.0 (100% Certain)

Audio Lesson: Classical vs Experimental Probability

Listen to the spoken lesson on formulas and probability classification.

1. Classical (Theoretical) Probability

Used when all outcomes in a sample space are equally likely to occur.

P(E) = (Number of Favorable Outcomes) / (Total Number of Possible Outcomes)

Example: Rolling a fair 6-sided die to get a 5: Favorable = 1, Total = 6. Thus P(5) = 1/6 ≈ 16.67%.

2. Experimental (Empirical) Probability

Based on actual trials, repeated experiments, or historical data.

P(E) = (Number of Times Event Occurred) / (Total Number of Trials Performed)

Example: Flipping a coin 100 times and getting Heads 54 times yields an experimental probability of P(H) = 54/100 = 54%.
